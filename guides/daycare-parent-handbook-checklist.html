Daycare Parent Handbook Checklist: What to Include

Works for any state — state-specific legal requirements are covered in our state licensing guides.

A parent handbook does two jobs at once: it sets expectations so you're not renegotiating your own policies with every family, and in most states, it's one of the first things a licensing inspector asks to see. Most providers either skip sections they don't realize matter, or write one so long parents never read it. Here's a complete checklist, organized the way inspectors and experienced directors actually think about it.

Core sections every handbook needs

  • Program philosophy & welcome — your approach to care, ages served, license type and number
  • Enrollment & registration — required documents, fees, waitlist policy
  • Hours, fees & payment policy — including late pickup fees and holiday closures
  • Health & illness policy — exclusion criteria, medication rules, return-to-care conditions
  • Discipline & behavior guidance — most states require an explicit no-corporal-punishment statement
  • Emergency & safety procedures summary — a plain-language version of your full emergency plan
  • Communication & parent involvement — how and how often you'll update families
  • Termination / disenrollment policy — notice period and grounds for immediate dismissal

What most providers forget

  • Photo & social media consent — explicit opt-in/opt-out, not assumed
  • Mandatory reporting disclosure — parents should know staff are legally required reporters
  • Weather closure policy — how you'll communicate closures and whether tuition is owed
  • Authorized pickup procedure — what happens if someone not on the list arrives
  • Allergy & food policy — including what happens with food brought from home
  • Screen time policy — increasingly expected by parents, rarely required by regulation

State-specific note: Some of the sections above have exact legal wording requirements in certain states (for example, Texas requires specific assurances around discipline practices). Frequently asked questions

Does my state legally require a parent handbook?

Many states expect one as part of standard operating procedure even without a specific statute mandating the word "handbook" — check your state's licensing minimum standards, or see our state-specific guides.

How long should a parent handbook be?

Long enough to cover every section above clearly, short enough that parents actually read it — most run 8-15 pages. Use headers and short paragraphs over dense legal blocks.

Can I just copy another center's handbook?

Not safely — policies need to match your actual operations and your state's specific requirements. A generic copy can commit you to policies you don't actually follow, which is its own compliance risk.
