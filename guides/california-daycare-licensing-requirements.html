California Daycare Licensing Requirements: Complete 2026 Guide

Last verified July 2026 · Not legal advice — see sourcing notes throughout

Licensed child care in California is regulated by the California Department of Social Services (CDSS), Community Care Licensing Division (CCLD), under Title 22 of the California Code of Regulations, Division 12 (Child Care Facility Licensing Regulations). This guide covers what actually shows up during a CCLD inspection, with the exact regulatory sections cited so you can verify anything before relying on it — including one requirement that's genuinely different from other states, not just relabeled.

Regulations are amended periodically. Always confirm current standards at cdss.ca.gov/inforesources/child-care-licensing before making a compliance decision.

Who Regulates Childcare in California

CDSS licenses and monitors child care centers and family child care homes under Title 22, Division 12. Child development programs funded by the California Department of Education operate under a separate framework, Title 5, with its own staffing ratios — if you receive CDE funding, Title 5 rules apply instead of the general Title 22 ratios described below.

Daily Illness Screening & Isolation

✓ 22 CCR §101226.1 & §101226.2 — quoted directly

Under §101226.1, the licensee is responsible for ensuring that children with obvious symptoms of illness — including but not limited to fever or vomiting — are not accepted. No child can be accepted without direct contact between staff and the person bringing the child in, and that person must stay until the child is formally accepted.

Under §101226.2, every center must have a dedicated isolation area (not in the kitchen or general-use bathroom) equipped with a mat, cot, or bed, positioned so staff can still supervise. If a child becomes ill during the day, the parent or authorized representative must be notified immediately and asked to pick the child up as soon as possible.

Flagged for verification. Unlike Texas, California's regulation text we retrieved describes illness by observable symptoms ("obvious symptoms," "fever or vomiting") rather than a single numeric temperature threshold in the sections above. If your program wants a specific degree cutoff for staff to apply consistently, set it in consultation with your program's health consultant or pediatrician — don't assume a number from another state's rule applies here.

Disaster & Emergency Drill Requirements

✓ 22 CCR §101174 — quoted directly

This is the clearest point of contrast with other states: California requires a written disaster and mass casualty plan, but drills are required at least every six months — not monthly. Requirements under §101174 include:

  • A written disaster plan covering fire, flood, and earthquake response, exit routes, transportation, and relocation sites
  • Instruction for all children (age and ability permitting) and all personnel, including volunteers, in their role under the plan
  • Disaster drills conducted at least once every six months, documented, with records kept on file for at least one year

Staff Requirements

Background checks. Staff must receive fingerprint clearance through both the California Department of Justice and the FBI before working unsupervised with children (22 CCR §101217).

Health & safety training. At least one staff member trained in pediatric CPR and first aid (per Health and Safety Code §1596.866) must be present whenever children are at the center or offsite for center activities. Programs commonly cite a 15-hour health and safety training requirement tied to this same code section — confirm the current hour count with CDSS, as we found this figure in secondary compliance summaries rather than quoted directly from the primary text.

Immunizations. Staff and volunteers are generally required to be immunized against influenza, pertussis, and measles (Health and Safety Code §1597.622), subject to applicable exemptions.

Discipline. California's regulations require discipline to be free of corporal punishment, physical pain, humiliation, or intimidation, with programs expected to use positive guidance and age-appropriate expectations.

Mandatory reporting. All staff are mandated reporters under California's Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting Act (CANRA, Penal Code §11164 et seq.) and must report suspected abuse or neglect to local law enforcement or the county child protective agency — California does not route this through a single statewide hotline the way some states do, so confirm your county's reporting line as part of onboarding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a California license the same as being licensed under Title 5?

No. Title 22 is the baseline licensing requirement for all licensed centers and homes. Title 5 applies specifically to programs funded by the California Department of Education, like the California State Preschool Program, and can carry different ratio and staff qualification rules.

How often are California centers inspected?

CCLD conducts both scheduled and unannounced inspections; exact frequency depends on facility type and compliance history.

Do I need a disaster plan before I'm licensed?

Yes — CCLD requires a written disaster and mass casualty plan as part of the licensing process itself, not something added after opening.
